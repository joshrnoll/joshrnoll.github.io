Ever feel like everyone is doing cool stuff with AI agents and you don't even know where to start?

Yeah. If I were starting over tomorrow, this is the order I'd do it in.

## Connect your agent to things

MCP is basically USB for agents. You plug your agent into the tools you already live in, and it can actually do things instead of just talking about them.

For me that's Gmail, Google Calendar, and Notion. The stuff I already open every day anyway.

One caution, and I mean this: only grant the access you're comfortable with. An agent that can read your email is useful. An agent with more access than you meant to give it is a different story. Start narrower than you think you need.

## Make it your primary interface

This is the habit change, and it's the step most people skip.

No more opening Gmail and clicking compose. You tell your agent, "I need to draft an email to X about Y," and you let it start the work.

I'll be honest: it felt slower at first. (Years of muscle memory don't just politely step aside because you read a blog post.) I'd catch myself mid-click, close the tab, and go back to the agent like I'd been caught cheating.

It wasn't actually slower. I was just used to clicking things.

Force it anyway. That's when it starts to click.

## Use skills to dial it in

Skills are just written-down versions of "what right looks like."

Every time the agent gets something almost right, capture the fix. What was off. What you wanted instead. The little preferences you'd otherwise re-explain tomorrow. That becomes a skill. Rinse and repeat until it stops needing the same correction.

This is the same idea as commander's intent, if you've heard me talk about that: get the intent down clearly, and even a messy prompt works a lot better.

## Automate

Once it does the thing well, stop asking every time.

Put it on a schedule or a trigger. Let it run while you're still drinking coffee.

That's the fun part. Reckless automation complete. Time for coffee.
